# Evaluation and learning model

## Universal signals

- Clear answer to actual question
- Practical steps, not slogans
- Ownership, decision criteria, governance and boundaries
- Stakeholder and team impact
- Evidence and honesty about limits
- Clear benefit to the target role
- Concise natural spoken delivery

## Per-answer coaching assessment

For coached `general`, `targeted` and `drill` interviews, **default to a consistent, substantive evaluation after each independently given answer**:

1. **Gesamtbewertung: x/10** — a qualitative, role-relative judgment with a brief reason; never present it as an objective hiring probability.
2. **Einzelbewertung** — select roughly 3–6 criteria that actually matter for this question, role, seniority and interviewer context; give each a 1–10 rating and, where useful, a short explanation. Do not use a universal fixed rubric or calculate the overall score as a mechanical average.
3. **Was besonders gut war** — identify concrete strengths supported by the candidate's actual words.
4. **Was ich verbessern würde** — identify missing evidence, unclear ownership, weak structure or other relevant omissions, and state how to improve them.
5. **Bessere gesprochene Antwort** — when useful, provide a concise natural spoken alternative grounded exclusively in established candidate facts, explicitly distinguishing a suggested formulation from factual evidence.

Choose the depth and number of criteria to match the substance of the answer. Avoid filler, redundant criteria and artificially precise scoring. If an answer is too incomplete to assess fairly, state what is missing and ask for clarification rather than inventing a confident score. Assess the **candidate's original answer**, not the assistant's rewrite. Record the assessment and supporting answer excerpts in the run.

In uninterrupted `mock` mode, defer this evaluation until the interview ends. Briefing mode does not score answers.

## Scoring interpretation

A score is a qualitative estimate of how well the answer would serve **this particular role and question**, not a generic personal rating. For leadership roles, relevant criteria may include ownership, judgment, people leadership, stakeholder alignment, outcomes and reflection. For engineering roles, they may include technical correctness, diagnosis, trade-offs, verification and clarity. Interviewer roles may inform question emphasis but must not be treated as known personal preferences.

## Competency IDs

- C01: Concrete implementation – two or three sequential steps, owner, next action
- C02: Role-value articulation – role-specific benefit backed by examples
- C03: Governance/decision ownership – consult, evaluate, decide or escalate through proper authority
- C04: Conflict follow-through – acknowledge, contain, investigate, communicate and review
- C05: Technical/product judgment – risk, operability, capacity, debt, value
- C06: Role transition and learning – honest gaps, credible learning path and motivation
- C07: Communication – concise, professional, non-defensive and grounded
- C08: Genuine behavioral evidence – situation, own action, verified result/learning

Statuses `open | improving | stable`. Baseline is not improvement. Move to `improving` only after two spontaneous successful answers in distinct scenarios; to `stable` only after confirmation in a subsequent run without coaching. Do not use the assistant's rewritten example as evidence.

## Separation

A missing domain-specific skill, compensation mismatch or commute preference is a role-fit issue, not an interview communication weakness. Record it under job preparation, not in candidate competency status.
