# Evaluation and learning model

## Universal signals

- Clear answer to actual question
- Practical steps, not slogans
- Ownership, decision criteria, governance and boundaries
- Stakeholder and team impact
- Evidence and honesty about limits
- Clear benefit to the target role
- Concise natural spoken delivery

## Block assessment (coached modes)

For coached `general`, `targeted` and `drill` interviews, evaluate **once a question block is closed** (`followup.md`), never after each single answer. If an essential requirement of the question is still open after an answer, the next turn is the neutral follow-up only: no score, no strengths/improvement list, no solution hint, no model answer. A block is closed when evidence suffices, no useful further clarification is expected, or the candidate does not know more. A complete answer needs no completion follow-up, and closing the block is not forced at once: ask at most one meaningful `deepening` follow-up first (no rating, hint or solution before it; no obligation; no quota); if no deepening is useful, close the block and evaluate it.

At block close give:

1. **Gesamtbewertung: x/10** — a qualitative, role-relative judgment with a brief reason; never an objective hiring probability.
2. **Einzelbewertung** — roughly 3–6 criteria that matter for this question, role, seniority and interviewer context, each 1–10 with a short explanation. No universal fixed rubric; the overall score is not a mechanical average. Rate only aspects the question explicitly asked for or that are essential to it.
3. **Was besonders gut war** — concrete strengths from the candidate's actual words.
4. **Was ich verbessern würde** — missing evidence, unclear ownership, weak structure; how to improve.
5. **Bessere gesprochene Antwort** — when useful, a concise natural alternative grounded only in established candidate facts, marked as suggestion; it may only rearrange what the candidate said (no added self-descriptions, tools, numbers or outcomes); mark missing details as `[ergänzen: …]`.

Stay with what the candidate said: never state that something is missing or open when the candidate did say it, and never attribute motives, purposes, feelings or effects the candidate did not state (e.g. "damit sich keine Seite als Verlierer fühlt"); suggestions are marked as suggestions. Do not rate style aspects (e.g. brevity) nobody asked for. Choose depth to match the answer; avoid filler and artificial precision. If, after the block ended, the answer remains too incomplete to assess fairly, state concretely what is missing; a numeric score is then optional and, if given, provisional and cautious. A missing **optional** element (e.g. a real example requested "wenn möglich" for a hypothetical scenario) is never an essential gap: do not ask for it as if required, do not deduct for it, and do not make it mandatory later in the block. Assess the **candidate's original answer and additions**, not the assistant's rewrite. Record the assessment and supporting excerpts in the run.

In uninterrupted `mock` mode, defer all evaluation until the interview ends (follow-ups are still allowed). Briefing mode does not score answers.

## Follow-up blocks

A confirmed role preference that argues against the role is not scored: no criterion row for it, no lowered overall score because of it and no score reason built on it (missing enthusiasm for, or reasons to take, the declined role); it is a job-specific fit point (`followup.md`). **Evidence fidelity:** before writing "fehlt", "nicht gesagt", "nicht genannt" or "offen", compare the claim with every candidate statement of the block (first answer and additions). A short statement counts: "mit beiden Teams durchgehen" says the teams are involved, so team involvement is not missing; at most recommend more depth ("zusätzlich möglich: wie Positionen angehört werden, was bei anhaltender Uneinigkeit passiert") as a recommendation, never as a gap or error, and never drop what was said. Likewise "ich … entscheide" / "als Verantwortliche entscheiden" says who decides, so "wer sie am Ende trifft bleibt offen" is false; at most ask for the criteria if the question asked for them. Write each "fehlt/offen" claim only after quoting to yourself the candidate sentence closest to it. Aspects only you raised in an add-on question are not gaps. **Examples:** a missing example is never a deduction and never a required improvement unless the question explicitly asked for one ("nennen Sie ein Beispiel", behavioral/STAR questions) or it is essential; no "Konkretheit 5/10, es fehlt ein Beispiel" for a question that did not ask for one — at most an optional recommendation ("optional: ein Beispiel würde die Aussage stützen") that appears neither in the score reasons nor in the stored assessment as a gap nor as a mandatory next-training item. **Attributions:** describe only what was said; no inferred habits, motives, degrees or contrasts ("denkt von Anfang an mit", "immer", "proaktiv", "bewusst", "vorab statt erst nach der Entscheidung", "und nicht erst im Nachhinein") that the candidate did not state. Statements about the course of this dialogue (the candidate answered after the follow-up, independently, without a hint from you) are facts of the dialogue and allowed.


Decision at the end of every answer: (1) an essential requirement is open, ambiguous or contradictory → one neutral follow-up, no rating yet; (2) all essential requirements are covered → no completion follow-up; ask at most one meaningful `deepening` follow-up (no deduction, no rating or solution before it, no obligation, no quota) or, if none is useful, close the block and rate it now; (3) in `mock` rate only at the end.


When a block had follow-ups (`followup.md`), rate the first answer and the additions visibly apart and then the block as a whole: spontaneous, concretized independently after a follow-up, still open, needed hints/coaching. The number of follow-ups alone never lowers a score; `deepening` is not an error; an unanswered optional ("wenn möglich") example is not a deduction. An answer after a neutral follow-up is the candidate's own evidence but not a spontaneously complete first answer; an answer after content hints is supported. Follow-ups and retries do not simulate additional independent scenarios for improving/stable. Only explicitly requested or question-essential aspects are rated.

## Scoring interpretation

A score is a qualitative estimate of how well the answer would serve **this particular role and question**, not a generic personal rating. For leadership roles, relevant criteria may include ownership, judgment, people leadership, stakeholder alignment, outcomes and reflection. For engineering roles, they may include technical correctness, diagnosis, trade-offs, verification and clarity. Interviewer roles may inform question emphasis but must not be treated as known personal preferences.

## Competency IDs

- C01: Concrete implementation – two or three sequential steps, owner, next action
- C02: Role-value articulation – role-specific benefit backed by examples
- C03: Governance/decision ownership – consult, evaluate, decide or escalate through proper authority
- C04: Conflict follow-through – acknowledge, contain, investigate, communicate and review
- C05: Technical/product judgment – risk, operability, capacity, debt, value
- C06: Role transition and learning – honest gaps, credible learning path and motivation
- C07: Communication – concise, professional, non-defensive and grounded
- C08: Genuine behavioral evidence – situation, own action, verified result/learning

Statuses `open | improving | stable`. Baseline is not improvement. Move to `improving` only after two spontaneous successful answers in distinct scenarios; to `stable` only after confirmation in a subsequent run without coaching. Do not use the assistant's rewritten example as evidence.

## Separation

A missing domain-specific skill, compensation mismatch, commute preference or a confirmed preference that does not match the role (e.g. hands-on implementation vs. a Product role) is a role-fit issue, not an interview communication weakness. Record it under job preparation, not in candidate competency status.
